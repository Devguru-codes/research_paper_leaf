\subsection{In-domain accuracy is saturated}
On the de-duplicated, grouped protocol every model on every backbone reaches 99.9--100\% clean
accuracy and $\ge98.5\%$ accuracy under any rotation (\cref{tab:main}). The benchmark therefore
cannot separate architectures on clean accuracy, which motivates the robustness and transfer probes
below. None of the structural modules costs clean accuracy.

\subsection{Transfer to field photographs}
\Cref{fig:crossdomain} shows the main effect. With ConvNeXt-T, which is trained on studio images
only, \SVA{} raises 20-way accuracy on raw field photographs from 33.5\% (plain) to 49.3\%
(+15.8 points, 15/15 fold--seed pairs, $p<10^{-3}$) and on auto-masked photographs from 35.1\% to
43.1\% (+8.0, 15/15, $p<10^{-4}$). Fusing the same valuations globally gives no gain (33.7\% raw), so
the improvement is not explained by the presence of shape features alone. Against the localised
controls the picture is more nuanced. \SVA{} beats slab-only attention by $+3.6$ points on raw
photographs (12/15, $p=0.003$), but its lead over strip pooling ($+1.5$, 10/15, $p=0.15$) is not
significant when all models are trained with rotation augmentation.

The gains do not carry over to stronger backbones. With ViT-S, slab-only attention is best on raw
photographs (64.8\% vs.\ 60.4\% plain), and \SVA{} is level with plain (60.7\%). BioCLIP~2 reaches
81.4\% on raw photographs without any module, about 30 points above the best ImageNet ConvNeXt
model, and all five variants lie within 0.5 points of each other. A biology foundation model thus
closes most of the studio-to-field gap on its own, and structural pooling adds nothing measurable
on top of it.

\subsection{Learning without rotation augmentation}
\label{sec:norot}
To test which robustness is built in rather than learned from augmented data, we retrain plain,
strip, slab-only and \SVA{} ConvNeXt-T models without rotation augmentation (\cref{fig:rotation}).
All three localised modules remain far more rotation-robust than the plain model (worst-case
accuracy 97.6--98.3\% vs.\ 93.8\%), and \SVA{} is not more rotation-robust than slab-only attention
(98.3\% each). On field photographs, however, \SVA{} keeps almost all of its transfer gain without
augmentation (48.6\% raw vs.\ 49.3\% with augmentation), whereas strip pooling drops from 47.8\% to
42.7\%. Without augmentation \SVA{} exceeds strip pooling by $+5.8$ (raw) and $+4.0$ (masked) points
and slab-only attention by $+5.2$ (raw) points, winning 5/5 folds in each case. The object-centred
sweep with measured valuations thus provides, by construction, transfer that image-axis strips only
acquire through augmentation. \NOROTSEEDS

\subsection{Corruptions and cue conflict}
On MedLeaf-C, both slab-based models and strip pooling improve ConvNeXt-T by 6.5--8.3 points over
plain and global fusion (\cref{fig:corruptions}); slab-only attention is best (89.4\%) and \SVA{} is
within one point (88.6\%). The gains come from noise, blur, JPEG and low-resolution corruptions, i.e.\
from degraded texture; colour, occlusion and background corruptions are near ceiling for all models.
For ViT-S and BioCLIP~2 all variants are within 0.5 points. Shape bias remains low everywhere
(7--14\%): when a silhouette is filled with another species' lamina, all models mostly follow the
lamina. \SVA{} raises ConvNeXt-T shape bias from 6.5\% to 9.0\% (5/5 folds), the same as strip
pooling, and BioCLIP~2 is the most shape-biased backbone (14\%). We therefore do not claim that
\SVA{} makes models shape-based; it makes ImageNet CNN features less brittle under texture
degradation and domain shift.

\subsection{What drives the gain: ablations}
\label{sec:abl}
\Cref{tab:abl} varies one ingredient at a time (ConvNeXt-T, seed 0, folds 0--2; differences below
about 3 points are within seed noise, cf.\ the seed std in \cref{tab:main}). Three ingredients matter.
(i)~\emph{Learned, appearance-conditioned cell weights}: with unit weights, i.e.\ pure mask geometry,
raw field accuracy falls from 53.1\% to 43.5\%; permuting the learned weights within the leaf costs
2.3 points, so their location carries part of the signal. (ii)~\emph{A fine sweep}: halving
directions and slabs costs 6.3 points. (iii)~\emph{Both structural fields}: blade-only and vein-only
variants lose 4.7 and 2.4 points. By contrast, restricting attention to the slab is \emph{not}
essential once the tokens carry valuations: attention over all leaf patches loses only 1.1 points.
The co-registered attention contributes mainly in the slab-only model, where the slab is the only
structural information. Finally, the choice of valuation matters. Euler tokens and the full Hadwiger
token transfer worse than area and perimeter alone (45.7\% and 44.3\% raw).

\subsection{Topology sensitivity}
The topology-destruction probe separates what the models respond to. Punching three interior holes
($\Delta\chi=-3$) changes predictions less than removing the same area as three bites from the
margin ($\Delta\chi=0$) for every ImageNet model; in a regression of the response on visual distance
and a topology-change indicator the indicator is negative (ConvNeXt-T: $-0.5$ to $-1.4$ log-units).
\SVA{} reacts most to margin bites (mean KL $35\times10^{-4}$ vs.\ $17$--$24\times10^{-4}$ for the
controls), consistent with tokens that measure boundary length. The only model that is more sensitive
to holes than to bites is the Euler-token variant (KL $46\times10^{-4}$ for holes, 4.5 times any
other model). The probe therefore confirms that the measured valuation shapes what the model is
sensitive to, and it shows that Euler sensitivity, while real, does not help species transfer.
Severing veins changes all models' predictions more than equal-size edits of vein-free lamina, by a
similar factor for every variant, so venation sensitivity is a property of the backbones rather than
of the module.
