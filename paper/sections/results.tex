\subsection{In-domain accuracy is saturated}
On the de-duplicated, grouped protocol every model on every backbone reaches 99.9--100\% clean
accuracy and $\ge98.5\%$ accuracy under any rotation (\cref{tab:main}). The studio benchmark therefore
cannot separate architectures, and none of the added modules costs clean accuracy. All further
results concern robustness and, primarily, transfer to field photographs.

\subsection{Decomposing the studio-to-field gain}
\label{sec:decomp}
\Cref{fig:crossdomain} and \cref{tab:contrasts} show the main result. A plain ConvNeXt-T trained on
studio images reaches 33.6\% on raw field photographs; the localised models reach 45.7--47.4\%, a gain
of 12--14 points (balanced accuracy: 31.4\% vs.\ 42.9--44.6\%). The ladder of matched models splits
this gain into three parts.

\emph{Mid-level features.} An extra branch that averages mid-level patch features over the whole
image, without any mask, adds $+3.3$ points on raw photographs (12/15 runs, not significant) and
$+4.6$ on masked ones (15/15, Holm $p=0.007$).

\emph{Leaf location.} Averaging the same branch over the leaf mask instead adds a further $+4.4$
points on raw photographs (15/15, Holm $p=0.001$) but only $+1.3$ on masked ones (n.s.). Knowing where
the leaf is thus matters when the background is present and hardly at all when it has been whitened,
which identifies this part as discounting the field background. On whitened photographs, the gain of
mask pooling over plain is almost entirely the mid-level-feature effect.

\emph{Localisation.} Pooling locally within the leaf adds another 4.6--6.2 points on raw photographs:
strip pooling $+5.7$ (25/25, Holm $p=0.002$) and slab-only attention $+4.6$ (24/25, Holm $p=0.006$)
are significant; \SVA{} $+6.2$ (23/25) is not after correction (Holm $p=0.11$) because its run-to-run
variance is the largest. On masked photographs localisation adds 1.2--1.6 points (n.s.).

\emph{Measured geometry} adds little if anything. Fusing the valuations globally, without
localisation, is equivalent to plain within $\pm3$ points ($-0.1$, 13/25; equivalence $p<10^{-3}$ on
raw and $p=0.006$ on masked photographs). Attached to localised tokens, they give $+0.5$ over strip
pooling (12/25) and $+1.7$ over slab-only attention (16/25) on raw photographs; neither is significant,
and the intervals exclude large benefits but not benefits of up to 3--5 points (equivalence within
$\pm3$ is shown only for \SVA{} vs.\ strip on masked photographs). Object-centred sweep slabs and
image-axis strips perform the same. Per species, \SVA{} beats plain on 17 of the 20 species and strip
pooling on 8 (\cref{tab:fieldspecies}). In the planned family, each localised model beats plain by
12--14 points on raw and about 7 on masked photographs (25/25 runs, Holm $p<10^{-3}$). The two-way
bootstrap agrees with the corrected test on every significant contrast.

\subsection{The gains are not an artefact of augmentation}
\label{sec:norot}
\label{sec:strongaug}
\emph{Without rotation augmentation} (5 folds $\times$ 5 seeds), the localised models remain far more
rotation-robust than plain (worst-case accuracy 97.5--98.4\% vs.\ 93.2\%; \cref{fig:rotation}) and
keep their field gain: plain 32.1\%, slab-only 43.2\%, strip 43.9\%, \SVA{} 45.5\% on raw photographs
(\SVA{} vs.\ plain $+13.5$ [$+9.7$, $+17.3$], 25/25). \SVA{} is again not distinguishable from strip
pooling ($+1.6$, 16/25) or slab-only attention ($+2.4$, 20/25). With a single seed these leads had
looked much larger ($+5.8$ and $+5.2$), which illustrates why we rely on many seeds. Mask pooling, trained on seeds 0--2 of this arm, reproduces the decomposition: it adds $+8.2$ [$+4.5$, $+11.9$] points over plain on raw photographs (15/15), and localised pooling adds a further $+3.7$ to $+7.3$ (14--15/15) on raw but no significant gain on masked photographs.

\emph{With strong augmentation} (background compositing, blur, noise; 3 seeds; \cref{tab:strongaug}),
every model improves: plain rises from 33.6\% to 42.0\% on raw photographs and MedLeaf-C reaches
96--98\%. Both architectural parts still add on top. Mask pooling gives $+8.0$ [$+4.7$, $+11.3$] over
plain (15/15), so training on composited backgrounds does not replace knowing where the leaf is, and
localisation adds a further $+3.3$ (strip, 15/15) to $+5.2$ (\SVA{}, 15/15) on raw photographs (none on
masked ones). \SVA{} with strong augmentation reaches 55.2\%, the best field accuracy of any ImageNet
model here, but is again not distinguishable from strip pooling ($+1.9$, 13/15). The architectural
and data-side remedies are therefore complementary.

\subsection{The gains depend on the backbone}
On EfficientNet-B0 (3 seeds) all four structural models gain only 1.5--3 points on raw photographs
(plain 26.8\%, \SVA{} 29.8\%, $+3.0$ [$-2.6$, $+8.6$], 11/15) and nothing on masked ones. On ViT-S,
localised pooling may still help (strip 62.7\%, slab-only 64.8\% vs.\ plain 60.4\%, one seed), but
\SVA{} does not (60.7\%). BioCLIP~2 reaches 81.4\% on raw photographs without any module, about
30 points above the best ImageNet ConvNeXt-T model, and all variants lie within 0.5 points of each
other. A biology foundation model thus closes most of the studio-to-field gap on its own.
The advantage is specific to biological pre-training rather than to large-scale pre-training as such: a DINOv2 ViT-S/14, self-supervised on 142M general images, reaches only 52.5\% (one seed), and on it mask pooling hurts ($48.0\%$) while strip pooling is neutral ($53.0\%$). For both
ViTs, masked photographs are 8--22 points \emph{harder} than raw ones, whereas for the CNNs the two
differ by at most about five points; the whitened background and the masker's errors apparently remove context these models
use, so we treat raw photographs as the primary field test.

\subsection{Corruptions and cue conflict}
On MedLeaf-C (\cref{fig:corruptions}) the structural models are more robust on ConvNeXt-T (plain 82.1\%,
patch pooling 83.8\%, mask pooling 84.8\%, strip 87.5\%, \SVA{} 87.0\%, slab-only 88.8\%), with the gains
coming from noise, blur, JPEG and low-resolution corruptions; colour, occlusion and background
corruptions are near ceiling for all models, and on the other backbones all variants are within about
one point. This comparison favours the mask-based models, however: under corruption they receive the
\emph{true} leaf mask, while the plain and patch-pooling models receive none. Its mask-free part, patch
pooling over plain ($+3.2$ points on seed 0), is about as large as the further step from mask pooling to
localised pooling, so we read MedLeaf-C as consistent with, but not evidence for, a texture-robustness
mechanism. Shape bias remains low everywhere (7--14\%): when a silhouette is filled with another
species' lamina, all models mostly follow the lamina. Localised pooling raises ConvNeXt-T shape bias
only slightly (plain 6.5\%, strip 9.1\%, \SVA{} 9.0\%; seed 0), and BioCLIP~2 is the most shape-biased
backbone (14\%). The localised models are therefore not shape-based.


\subsection{Inside \SVA{}: ablations}
\label{sec:abl}
\Cref{tab:abl} varies one ingredient of \SVA{} at a time (ConvNeXt-T). A single-seed screen on folds
0--2 (lower block) was followed by the four most informative ablations on all 5 folds $\times$ 3
seeds (upper block), compared with \SVA{} on the same runs. The screen overstated every effect,
because its \SVA{} reference happened to be strong (53.1\% raw vs.\ 49.3\% over all seeds). With
three seeds, a coarser sweep costs $5.1$ [$1.1$, $9.1$] raw points (15/15) and unit cell weights
$4.5$ [$-0.5$, $9.5$] (13/15); Euler tokens transfer slightly worse than area and perimeter ($-2.6$,
12/15, n.s.), although they are the only ConvNeXt-T tokens that make the model respond more to holes in
the blade than to margin edits (\cref{sec:topo}); and restricting attention to the slab is not needed (global attention 49.3\% vs.\
49.3\%). These effects concern the internal design of \SVA{}; as shown above, the full module is not
better than localised pooling without any measurement. The vein head reproduces its ridge-filter
supervision closely on held-out leaves (Pearson $r=0.96$, Dice $0.74$--$0.75$ inside the blade),
which measures agreement with the pseudo-label, not with traced veins.



\subsection{Leakage audit}
\label{sec:leak}
Under random image-level splits that keep the duplicates, 5--11 test images per fold (1.4--3.0\%) have
an exact duplicate or a re-shot of the same leaf in the training part; under our grouped protocol
there are none. On this dataset the leak does not measurably inflate accuracy: a DeepHerb-style
reproduction (Xception features with an RBF-SVM~\citep{roopashree2021deepherb}) reaches
$98.5\pm0.9\%$ with random splits and $98.4\pm0.7\%$ with grouped splits, the dual-branch RGB +
vein-skeleton baseline~\citep{dualbranch2026} reaches 99.95\% under both, and our plain and \SVA{}
models reach 100\% under random splits as under grouped ones. We therefore present the grouped protocol
as hygiene that removes a known leak rather than as evidence that earlier results were inflated; the
saturation itself is the more important observation.
