\section{Unit tests of the structural signals}
\label{app:tests}
All properties used in \cref{sec:method} are checked by automated tests that ship with the code
(32 tests, all passing).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Exact Euler characteristic.} On toy shapes (disk, annulus, two disks, a lens-shaped
  leaf, and the lens with two holes; $\chi=1,0,2,1,-1$) the cubical $\chi=V-E+F$ of the binary set
  equals the value computed independently with scikit-image (4-connected foreground).
\item \textbf{Telescoping.} For every direction, $\sum_k\dchi(\theta,k)$, $\sum_k\Delta A(\theta,k)$ and
  $\sum_k\Delta P(\theta,k)$ equal the total valuation of the shape, to numerical precision.
\item \textbf{Partition of unity.} Slab memberships~\eqref{eq:slab} sum to one for every height.
\item \textbf{Sharp limit.} With binary weights and large $\kappa$, the relaxed increments equal those
  of a hard sweep that assigns each cell to the slab containing its height.
\item \textbf{Closed form.} For an $n\times n$ block of pixels, the totals are
  $(A,P,\chi)=((n-1)^2,4(n-1),1)$.
\item \textbf{Consistency.} The Euler column of the Hadwiger increments equals the separately
  implemented Euler increments.
\item \textbf{Rotation.} For unit weights, rotating the input by $90^\circ$ cyclically shifts
  $\dchi$, $\Delta A$ and $\Delta P$ by $K/4$ directions (\cref{prop:equiv}).
\item \textbf{Fuzzy subcomplex.} Learned cell weights satisfy $w(\text{coface})\le w(\text{face})$, and
  gradients reach the per-pixel weights.
\item \textbf{Splits.} Under the grouped protocol no exact duplicate or re-shot group crosses a fold
  boundary and every image is tested exactly once; under the random protocol leaks occur.
\item \textbf{Conformal.} On synthetic exchangeable data, mean marginal coverage over 30 repetitions is at least
  $1-\alpha$ (up to 0.5 points). When a hazard tier's calibration set is too small for its target
  level $\alpha_\tau$, its threshold is infinite and all of its species are always included in the
  set, i.e.\ the procedure fails safe.
\end{itemize}

\section{Hazard-aware conformal sets}
\label{app:conformal}
We calibrate split-conformal APS sets~\citep{romano2020aps} (cf.\ hierarchical variants~\citep{hcc2025,hierarchicalcp2025}) on each fold's held-out
calibration split (245 images), Mondrian over hazard tiers, with $\alpha=0.10$ for all tiers
(standard) or $\alpha=0.10/0.05/0.01$ for tiers 0/1/2 (hazard-aware). Under exchangeability this
gives tier-wise coverage $\ge1-\alpha_\tau$; no guarantee is claimed under distribution shift.
Two limitations dominate. (i)~In-domain accuracy is saturated, so in-domain coverage is trivially
100\% (mean set size 1.6--2.7 species). (ii)~Each calibration split contains only about ten
\textit{Nerium} images; with $\alpha_2=0.01$ the tier-2 threshold is infinite, so oleander is
included in every set, and even with $\alpha=0.10$ it appears in 61\% of in-domain sets because label
smoothing keeps top-class probabilities near 0.9. On field photographs (no guarantee), standard sets
cover 86--92\% of true species for ConvNeXt-T and 95--97\% for BioCLIP~2 (raw photos), but they are
large (8--17 of 30 species, rarely resolving below order level). The sets therefore signal the shift
honestly, but a deployable hazard-aware layer needs more calibration data for toxic species than this
dataset provides. Full results are in \texttt{results/conformal.csv}.
