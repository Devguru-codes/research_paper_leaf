\begin{abstract}
Medicinal-leaf classifiers report near-perfect accuracy on studio photographs of single leaves, but
how they transfer to field photographs, and why, is largely unknown. We study this question on the
30-species Medicinal Leaf Dataset with a de-duplicated, leaf-grouped protocol and test on 20 species
of the DIMPSAR field photographs. Holding backbone, data and training fixed, we compare a plain
classifier with a ladder of additions: pooling over the leaf mask, localised pooling (image-axis
strips or object-centred sweep slabs), and \emph{sweep-valuation attention} (\SVA{}), a module we
introduce whose tokens additionally carry the area and boundary length that a learned,
appearance-weighted cubical complex of the blade and venation adds in each slab. Clean accuracy is
saturated for every model. On field photographs with ConvNeXt-T (5 folds $\times$ 5 seeds,
corrected resampled tests with Holm correction, and a bootstrap over runs and field species), the
transfer gain decomposes into two parts of similar size: knowing where the leaf is ($+7.5$ points over
33.6\%) and pooling locally within it ($+4.6$ to $+6.2$ more). Both parts persist without rotation
augmentation and add to a strong background-compositing augmentation (best model 55\%). Measured
geometry adds nothing detectable on top of localised pooling: \SVA{} (47.4\%) is statistically tied
with strip pooling (46.8\%) and with its own slab-only variant, and fusing the same measurements
globally gives no gain at all. The localisation gain is small on EfficientNet-B0 and absent on ViT-S
and on the BioCLIP~2 foundation model, which reaches 81\% without any module. We release the protocol,
a corruption suite, cue-conflict and topology probes, all code and every run output at
\url{https://github.com/Devguru-codes/research_paper_leaf}.
\end{abstract}
