\begin{abstract}
Medicinal-leaf classifiers report near-perfect accuracy on studio photographs of single leaves, but
how they transfer to field photographs, and why, is largely unknown. We study this on the 30-species
Medicinal Leaf Dataset with a de-duplicated, leaf-grouped protocol, testing on 2{,}000 DIMPSAR field
photographs of 20 shared species. Holding backbone, data and training fixed, we climb a ladder of
matched models: a plain classifier, an extra branch on mid-level features, the same branch pooled over
the leaf mask, localised pooling over image strips or object-centred sweep slabs, and
\emph{sweep-valuation attention} (\SVA{}), a module we introduce whose slab tokens also carry the area
and boundary length of learned, appearance-weighted cubical complexes of the blade and venation. Clean
accuracy is saturated for every model. On raw field photographs, ConvNeXt-T (5 folds $\times$ up to 5
seeds, corrected resampled tests, Holm correction) gains 12--14 points over a plain model (33.6\%). The
gain has three parts: mid-level features ($+3.3$, not significant), knowing where the leaf is ($+4.4$,
which vanishes once the background is whitened) and pooling locally within the leaf ($+4.6$ to $+6.2$).
Leaf location and localisation persist under strong background-compositing augmentation, which raises
every model (best 55\%) but replaces neither. Measured geometry adds little if anything: \SVA{}
(47.4\%) is statistically tied with strip pooling (46.8\%) and its own measurement-free variant, and
fusing the same measurements globally is equivalent to the plain model within $\pm3$ points. The
gains are small on EfficientNet-B0 and absent on ViTs; the BioCLIP~2 foundation model reaches 81\%
without any module, whereas a general self-supervised DINOv2 ViT reaches 52\%. Code, all run outputs and the probes are released at
\url{https://github.com/Devguru-codes/research_paper_leaf}.
\end{abstract}
