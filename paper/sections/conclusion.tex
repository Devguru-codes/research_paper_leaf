\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Medicinal-leaf classifiers that are perfect on studio photographs transfer poorly to field
photographs. In a controlled study with matched models, five seeds and corrected tests, we find that
for an ImageNet CNN the architectural part of the remedy is simple: pool over the leaf, and pool
locally within it. Each step is worth 5--8 points on field photographs, and both add to
background-compositing augmentation. Measured leaf geometry, introduced here as sweep-valuation
attention with appearance-weighted area, boundary and Euler measurements along an object-centred
sweep, does not improve transfer beyond localised pooling, and a biology foundation model makes all
such modules unnecessary. We release the leakage-audited protocol, the field mapping, the corruption,
cue-conflict and topology probes, and all code and run outputs.
