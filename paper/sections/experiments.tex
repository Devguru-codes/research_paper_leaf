\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Backbones and training.} We use two ImageNet-1k CNNs (ConvNeXt-T and
EfficientNet-B0), ViT-S/16 (AugReg, ImageNet-21k$\to$1k) and the BioCLIP~2 ViT-L/14 visual tower (all
but the last two blocks frozen). All models of a backbone share the recipe: AdamW (new modules lr
$10^{-3}$, weight decay $0.05$), 2 warm-up epochs then cosine decay, batch 32, mixed precision, label
smoothing $0.1$. The backbone lr is $10^{-4}$ for ConvNeXt-T, $2\times10^{-4}$ for EfficientNet-B0 and
$5\times10^{-5}$ for ViT-S and $3\times10^{-5}$ for DINOv2 ViT-S/14~\citep{oquab2024dinov2}, all trained 40 epochs at $384^2$; BioCLIP~2 uses lr $3\times10^{-5}$,
20 epochs and $224^2$ input. The recipe was fixed in advance and not tuned per model. Augmentation
uses uniform rotation in $[-180^\circ,180^\circ]$, scale $[0.85,1.15]$, translation $\pm5\%$,
horizontal flips and mild colour jitter, applied jointly to image, mask and vein pseudo-label. No
early stopping or test-time augmentation is used. Runs use Kaggle T4 GPUs.

\paragraph{Runs.} All splits are grouped stratified 5-fold cross-validation over the de-duplicated,
leaf-grouped data (\cref{sec:data}). For ConvNeXt-T, plain, patch pooling, mask pooling, strip, slab-only, \SVA{}
and global fusion are trained with 5 seeds per fold (25 runs each); the field test is run on all seeds, MedLeaf-C on several seeds but
reported on seed 0, the one seed run for every model, and the other probes on seed 0. EfficientNet-B0 uses 3 seeds per fold, ViT-S,
BioCLIP~2 and DINOv2 ViT-S/14 one seed per fold. Two further ConvNeXt-T arms test whether the effects
depend on augmentation. \emph{Without rotation augmentation}: plain, strip, slab-only and \SVA{}
(5 seeds) and mask pooling (3 seeds). \emph{Strong augmentation}: plain, mask pooling, strip and
\SVA{}, 3 seeds, with background compositing (with probability 0.8 the white background is replaced
by a random procedural texture, blended through the slightly eroded mask), Gaussian blur ($p=0.3$)
and Gaussian noise ($p=0.3$) on top of the standard augmentation.

\paragraph{Statistics.} The primary outcome is 20-way accuracy on raw DIMPSAR field photographs; the
masked photographs are the secondary outcome. Each contrast is the mean difference over matched
fold--seed runs, with a 95\% interval from the Nadeau--Bengio corrected resampled
$t$-test~\citep{nadeau2003inference} (test/train ratio $0.3$, accounting for overlapping training
sets). The study was not pre-registered, and we separate two families of contrasts, each
Holm-corrected~\citep{holm1979simple} over both photo types. The \emph{planned} family was fixed
before any run: each localised model vs.\ plain, \SVA{} vs.\ slab-only and vs.\ strip pooling
(measured geometry), and global fusion vs.\ plain (12 tests). The \emph{post-hoc} family uses the
patch- and mask-pooling controls, which we added after a first analysis to test a suspected confound
(10 tests); these contrasts confirm or refute that confound rather than test a prior hypothesis. A
difference is called significant only if its Holm-corrected $p<0.05$. For the geometry and
global-fusion contrasts, where the question is whether an ingredient helps at all, we also test
equivalence within $\pm3$ accuracy points with two one-sided corrected $t$-tests. As a robustness
check, a two-way bootstrap that resamples runs and field species (clusters of images) jointly also
captures sampling of the fixed field test set. Secondary comparisons (other arms, backbones, probes)
are reported with the corrected interval and win counts, without multiplicity claims.
\paragraph{Ablations of \SVA{}.} Euler-only and full-Hadwiger tokens; blade-only and vein-only
fields; unit cell weights; \emph{permuted} learned weights (the same weight values shuffled within the
leaf before computing the valuations); global instead of slab-restricted attention; a coarser sweep
($K{=}16$, $T{=}8$); and a softer or sharper relaxation ($\kappa\in\{1,16\}$). All ablations run on
seed 0, folds 0--2; the four most informative (unit weights, global attention, coarse sweep, Euler
token) are repeated on all five folds and seeds 0--2 and compared with \SVA{} on the same runs.

\paragraph{Probes.} All probes are applied to each fold's test images with fixed seeds.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Field photographs}: models trained on the studio images are tested on the 2{,}000 DIMPSAR
photographs of the 20 overlapping species (\cref{app:field}), on raw crops with the background kept
and on crops whose background was whitened by our masker. Accuracy is 20-way, i.e.\ restricted to the
shared species; this uses the knowledge of which species can occur and is the same for all models.
\item \textbf{MedLeaf-C}: ten corruptions $\times$ three severities: Gaussian noise, blur, JPEG,
low resolution, darkening, contrast loss, hue shift (yellowing), desaturation (severity 3 is
grayscale), occluders, and pasted procedural/soil-like backgrounds. Masks are the oracle masks
transformed consistently.
\item \textbf{Rotation sweep}: accuracy at $0^\circ,30^\circ,\dots,330^\circ$.
\item \textbf{Cue conflict}: the silhouette of a test leaf $A$ is filled with the lamina of a test
leaf $B$ of another species. $B$ is aligned by principal axes and enlarged until it covers $A$, so
that $B$'s outline contributes no shape cue. Shape bias $=n_A/(n_A+n_B)$ where $n_A$ ($n_B$) counts
predictions of $A$'s ($B$'s) species.
\item \textbf{Topology destruction}: three interior holes in the blade (exact $\Delta\chi=-3$,
verified) versus three boundary bites of equal removed area ($\Delta\chi=0$, verified), and inpainting
on strong veins versus vein-free lamina. Every edit records LPIPS, pixel RMSE and a colour-histogram
distance; the response (KL divergence between clean and edited predictions) is compared within LPIPS
strata (\cref{sec:topo}).
\end{itemize}
