\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What closes the studio-to-field gap.} For an ImageNet ConvNeXt-T trained on studio leaves,
the architectural gain on raw field photographs has three identifiable parts. An extra branch on
mid-level features helps a little (3.5 points, not significant on raw photographs, 4.4 on whitened
ones). Pooling that branch over the leaf adds about 4 points, and only when the field background is
present: on photographs whose background has been whitened it adds almost nothing, so this part is the
model discounting the background. Pooling locally within the leaf adds another 5--6 points on raw
photographs but little on whitened ones, and it barely changes shape bias. Why localisation helps
when clutter is present remains open; a plausible reading is that pooling small regions separately
keeps background that leaks through an imperfect mask from contaminating the whole leaf
representation, but we have not tested this. Mask pooling and localisation both survive strong
background-compositing augmentation, which lifts every model but replaces neither.

\paragraph{What does not.} We expected measured leaf geometry to matter: an object-centred sweep,
per-slab area and boundary length of the blade and venation, and attention restricted to the slab
being measured. Fusing the measurements globally is equivalent to the plain model within $\pm3$ points
(two one-sided tests, $p<0.01$ on both photo types). Attached to localised tokens, they improve on
strip pooling and slab-only attention by $+0.5$ and $+1.7$ points on raw photographs, with intervals
that exclude large benefits but not benefits of up to 3--5 points; object-centred slabs and
image-axis strips perform the same, and restricting attention to the measured slab can be replaced by
global attention. Within \SVA{}, a fine sweep and learned weights matter, but only relative to weaker
versions of \SVA{} itself, and Euler tokens make a model sensitive to holes in the blade yet transfer
worse. Within the resolution of our design, the effects we can detect concern \emph{where} features
are pooled, not measurements of the leaf. We report \SVA{} because a well-controlled negative result
for a natural and increasingly popular idea, fusing topological and geometric measurements with deep
features, is useful.
\paragraph{Practical recommendations.} (i)~Evaluate leaf classifiers on field photographs; studio
accuracy is saturated and uninformative (98--100\% for every model). (ii)~With an ImageNet CNN, pool
over the leaf and locally within it, e.g.\ with strip pooling, which is simpler and cheaper than \SVA{},
and combine it with background-compositing augmentation; together they raised field accuracy from
34\% to 53--55\%. Note that a leaf mask is needed at test time, for cropping and for these models; our
masker costs about as much as the classifier (\cref{app:cost}). (iii)~Where a biology foundation model such as
BioCLIP~2 can be used, use it: it reaches 81\% without any module, and none of the modules changes it.

\paragraph{Does it generalise?} The LeafSnap30 replication, with predictions fixed in advance,
supports the clutter account. Where field leaves lie on plain paper, the leaf-location effect
disappears, the total gain shrinks from 13 to 4 points, and measured geometry again adds nothing;
localised pooling stays ahead of mask pooling ($+2.5$) but not significantly. Localisation is thus
the less certain of the two ingredients outside DIMPSAR.

\paragraph{Limitations.} (i)~Two studio-to-field pairs, and only DIMPSAR has cluttered field
backgrounds; the medicinal pair has 20 shared species. Field
labels come from vernacular folder names that we mapped to species and checked visually but not with a
botanist; residual label noise would lower all models alike. The 20-way accuracy uses the knowledge of
which species can occur. The only high-hazard species of the studio set, \textit{Nerium oleander}, is
not among the field species, so we could not evaluate confusions with a toxic look-alike in the
field. (ii)~The field test set depends on our masker for every model: it centres the crops and excluded
5.6\% of the photographs, probably among the hardest, and the mask-based models also use its masks
inside the network. Its accuracy on field photographs could not be measured without ground-truth
masks. (iii)~ConvNeXt-T results use five seeds (LeafSnap30 two),
EfficientNet-B0 and the key ablations three, the ViTs one; differences of 2--3 points between
localised models are not resolved, and the ViT results are indicative only. (iv)~One fixed training
recipe was used for all models of a backbone; a recipe tuned per model could shift individual numbers.
(v)~The mask- and patch-pooling controls were added after a first analysis; on DIMPSAR their
contrasts confirm a suspected confound rather than test a pre-registered hypothesis (the LeafSnap30
replication was pre-registered). (vi)~The venation field is learned
from ridge-filter pseudo-labels and is not validated against traced veins.
