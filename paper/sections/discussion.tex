\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{When does structure help?} The clearest pattern in our results is a dependence on the
backbone. For an ImageNet CNN, measuring area and boundary length along an object-centred sweep, with
cell weights conditioned on appearance, turns a model that transfers poorly from studio to field
photographs (34\%) into one that transfers substantially better (49\%), and it does so without
needing rotation augmentation. For ViT-S the module is neutral, and for BioCLIP~2, whose
pre-training already covers millions of organism images, no structural module changes anything.
Practitioners with access to a biology foundation model should use it; the structural module is
useful where such a model is unavailable or too large, e.g.\ for on-device CNNs.

\paragraph{What the ablations say about the mechanism.} Our initial hypothesis was that
\emph{co-registration}, i.e.\ attending only within the slab whose geometry is measured, would be
the key ingredient. The ablations do not support this as the main driver: once the tokens carry
valuations, global attention performs within noise of slab-restricted attention. What matters is
(i)~appearance-conditioned weights, which let the network decide which parts of the blade and
venation count towards the measured geometry, and (ii)~a fine sweep. Restricting attention to the
slab matters when the slab is the \emph{only} structural information (slab-only model). We report
co-registration as a design choice of \SVA{} and not as the source of its gains.

\paragraph{Topology is measurable but not what transfers.} Euler-characteristic tokens make a
model demonstrably sensitive to holes in the blade, which none of the other models are, yet they
transfer worse to field photographs than metric valuations. For species identification the metric
profile of a leaf (how its width and margin length vary along each direction) is more informative and
more robust than its connectivity. This is consistent with botanical practice, where outline and
margin are primary characters, and with recent findings that leaf outlines are highly informative
of other leaf traits~\citep{ayub2026ectplants}.

\paragraph{Benchmark saturation.} Clean accuracy on this dataset is saturated even after removing
duplicates and grouping re-shots of the same leaf, and so are rotation robustness under rotation
augmentation and several corruption families. Reports of 98--100\% on studio leaf benchmarks should
therefore be read as a lower bar; transfer to field photographs remains far from solved
(49\% for the best ImageNet model and 82\% for BioCLIP~2 on 20 species).

\paragraph{Limitations.} (i)~One studio dataset and one field dataset with 20 shared species;
field labels come from vernacular folder names that we mapped and checked visually. (ii)~ViT-S and
BioCLIP~2 were run with one seed, ablations with one seed on three folds; effects below about
3 points in those settings are within seed noise. (iii)~The venation field is learned from
ridge-filter pseudo-labels and is not a validated vein segmentation. (iv)~Hazard tiers are an
explicit assumption table; the conformal layer (\cref{app:conformal}) is limited by the small
number of calibration images for toxic species and gives no guarantee under shift.
